3 · Recall / Precision / FP Trade-off

The candidate's detection head became more permissive. Two consequences follow directly and are both visible in the data:

  • Recall up, FN down (good). True positives held roughly flat (749,464 → 748,312) while false negatives fell 164,583 → 145,926. TP-rate rose +1.69 pts. Evidence: KPI snapshot, Class_rate_comparison.csv.
  • FP exploded (bad). False positives grew by 202,034 (+58.6%) and FP-rate rose +10.7 pts (31.5% → 42.2%). Precision fell 10.7 pts. Evidence: FP_diff_by_label.csv sums to +201,726 across 7 labels; KPI snapshot.
  • Net effect is negative. F1 (the balance of the two) dropped from 0.746 to 0.684. In a perception stack feeding planning/tracking, a 59% rise in false detections raises phantom-obstacle braking and tracker-association risk that the modest recall gain does not offset.

Because headline recall barely moved, a naive read ("recall is up, ship it") is misleading. The next sections show that beneath the stable-ish recall there is heavy object-level churn (16,606 objects recovered FN→TP and 14,466 lost TP→FN) and a pervasive FP increase.

4 · Distance-Range Analysis

Near / mid / far read. Near (0–40 m): recall slightly better, but FPR up +23–25 pts — the danger zone. Mid (40–110 m): recall better, FPR modestly up. Far (120 m+): essentially unchanged for both metrics. The false-positive damage is overwhelmingly a close-range problem, exactly where planning is most sensitive.

10 · Root-Cause Hypotheses — Evidence vs. Hypothesis

✓ Evidence-backed observations

  • Candidate B is uniformly more permissive: recall ↑ in every class and nearly every distance bin, FP ↑ in 632/707 datasets. Class_rate_comparison, Distance_rates, FP_diff_by_dataset
  • FP damage is near-range: FPR roughly doubles at 0–40 m and converges to baseline beyond ~90 m. Distance_rates, Per-class_distance_deltas
  • The FP rise is class-led by "unknown" (+128k, 63% of total) and broad, not concentrated (top 100 of 707 datasets = 44%). FP_diff_by_label, FP_diff_by_dataset
  • Whole-scene collapses exist: Omika_5b31d85d (−3,597 net TP, 0 improved / 3,597 degraded) and Kawasaki_3a0c82f1 (−2,274, 0 improved) lost essentially all tracked objects. Object_diff_by_scene
  • 953 near-range high-visibility objects flipped TP→FN, pedestrian+car = 88%; 60 objects lost for ≥10 consecutive frames. Critical_degraded_cases, Consecutive_degraded_objects

? Hypotheses (need inspection, not yet proven)

  • PTv3 branch lowers the effective score threshold / adds duplicate proposals, inflating near-range FP. Test: compare raw score distributions and NMS behavior on/off. hypothesis
  • The "unknown" FP surge may be misclassified real returns (clutter, vegetation, parked-object density) newly proposed as objects. Test: inspect unknown FP boxes in top Kawasaki/Komatsu scenes. hypothesis
  • Whole-scene collapses (Omika_5b31d85d, Kawasaki_3a0c82f1) may be a data/registration/timestamp fault for those datasets rather than a model quality issue — 0 improved / all degraded is atypical of a model change. Test: open the 3D viewer for those datasets first. hypothesis
  • Slightly worse yaw on two-wheelers may be a shared-feature side-effect of the PTv3 fusion. hypothesis
  • GT-count drift (−2.2%) suggests evaluation-set or filtering differences between runs; confirm both runs evaluate the identical frame set before trusting absolute counts. hypothesis

11 · Recommendation

Release judgment: BLOCK Candidate B (ptv3_on) is not ready to replace Baseline A. F1 regresses 6.3 pts on a +58.6% false-positive increase; the small recall gain does not compensate.

Must-check before any re-evaluation

  1. Triage the whole-scene collapses first — open Omika_5b31d85d and Kawasaki_3a0c82f1 in the 3D compare viewer (links in §6/§8). Confirm whether these are a model failure or a data/registration artifact; they alone account for ~5,900 net TP losses and skew the object-diff totals.
  2. Investigate the "unknown" FP source (+128k, 63% of the rise) in the worst Kawasaki/Komatsu datasets (§7 table). Determine if these are duplicate proposals, threshold changes, or genuine new clutter detections.
  3. Audit near-range FP / NMS. The doubling of FPR at 0–40 m points to a scoring or de-duplication change in the PTv3-on path. Compare raw confidence distributions and NMS settings A vs B.
  4. Review the 953 safety-critical FN — prioritize the ≥10-consecutive-frame pedestrian and car losses (§8 table), especially Fukuyama_3c11fea2 (car, 44 frames, 3.1 m).

Suggested next experiments

  • Re-run with the candidate's operating threshold re-tuned to match Baseline A's FP-rate, then re-compare F1 — this isolates "genuinely better detector" from "just more permissive".
  • Verify both runs evaluate the identical GT frame set (resolve the −2.2% GT drift) before trusting absolute TP/FP/FN.
  • Keep the one clear win — motorbike (recall +14.5, precision +47.4, F1 +55.3) — as a reference for what "good" looks like, and check why unknown/bicycle behave oppositely.
